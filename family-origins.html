Family origins

If I travel far enough back through my family history, the clearest path begins on my mother’s side.

The surviving family tree and the Ferenczy family memorial document the connection between the Cseuz and Ferenczy lines through Etelka Cseuz. What matters to me is not rank or inherited status, but the fact that making, designing and working by hand remained present across several generations.

My mother, Erzsébet Rózsa Győry, is an interior designer, painter, visual artist and natural therapist. My father, Gábor Bánhalmi, designed individual furniture as a wood-industry engineer. Kálmán Győry and his wife, Róza Margit Ferenczy, taught and painted in Sopron. My maternal great-grandfather, József Ferenczy, created geometric woven artworks.

László Győry, my mother’s brother, worked as a photographer in Sopron. He did not teach me photography and there was no master–student relationship between us. Photography was simply already present in the family story before me.

I did not inherit a finished artistic programme. I inherited an environment in which making something felt natural, and in which form, structure, material and the human being mattered at the same time.
Source background: family memorial, Csausz–Cseuz and Cseuz–Ferenczy family trees, and Norbert Bánhalmi’s family account. This archive presents the family line as concise background and does not claim a complete genealogical study.
