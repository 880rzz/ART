A literary anthology with photography · Period IV, 2019–2022

Why this book matters in the photographic oeuvre

Fabulous Book (original Hungarian title Mesés könyv) was published on 23 November 2019 as a 160-page hardcover anthology. Issued by newkids in Budapest, it contains four works by three writers. The Bookberry publisher and retailer record describes it as an LGBTQ-themed literary publication and explicitly credits Norbert Bánhalmi's photographs. It is important to distinguish the authors' texts from the photographer's separate contribution to the book's visual language.

Four texts, not a single authored narrative

The contents comprise Carol Souya's Maurice és a Rózsa, avagy a szívtelen herceg meséje, Becca Prior's Kölcsöncsalád, and two pieces by Hayden Moore: A Bukowski-projekt and Minden rendben…. Those titles and credits are documented in the publisher-related record; there is no need to invent plot summaries or assign the book one collective literary voice. Its structure brings distinct pieces of writing together in a physical volume accompanied by photography.

What Norbert Bánhalmi actually contributed

Norbert Bánhalmi is credited for photography, not for authorship of the literary stories. His images provide a parallel visual way into the anthology rather than converting its writers' work into his own. This is also what makes the book different from the exhibition-related publications of 2017 and 2018: here the photographer enters an independently authored literary and publishing collaboration.

Why it belongs to Period IV

The 2019–2022 chapter of the oeuvre concerns publication, institutional relationships and responsibility within collaborations. Mesés könyv provides a tangible record of that development: three named writers, the newkids publisher and a clearly attributed photographic contribution. The original Hungarian title, cover, 2019 publication date and ISBN 978-615-6030-04-7 preserve the bibliographic identity of the edition across translated archive pages.

Photography and literature

The photographs are part of the book's visual language, accompanying three writers' distinct stories. The texts are the authors' work; my role in the publication was photographic, not literary authorship.
